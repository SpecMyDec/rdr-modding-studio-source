# GLOSSARY — what the field names read as. **INFERENCE.**

Owner: **rdr-re**. Generated by
`03_Tools/research/probe_glossary.py`; do not hand-edit.

> **This file is not evidence.** `FIELDS.md` is measurement —
> kinds and ranges counted over every file on the disc. This one
> reads the NAMES, and a name can lie. Nothing here should be put
> in front of a user as fact without someone checking the field
> actually does what its name suggests.

## How it is kept honest

**The English comes from the game.** There is no dictionary on
the build machine, and inventing a word list would make this
unfalsifiable. 3708 words from three auditable sources:

- **3669 displayed** — the `string` column of the 38 `.xls` design
  spreadsheets and the text of all 101,778 `.strtbl` records.
- the 248-name **AI vocabulary** from the executable.
- **102 shared** — tokens used as a key token in at least 8
  different extensions. A term reused across eight unrelated file
  types is part of the engine's vocabulary.

The third source exists because of a measurement: with only the
first two, **66% of all keys came back OPAQUE**. The game's
displayed English is not a dictionary for its internal field
names — **39 of the shared terms are never shown to a player**,
`rate`, `period`, `blend` and `slew` among them.

**Abbreviations carry their evidence.** 40 expansions, 24 backed
by the long form occurring as a key token elsewhere or by
`guns.py`'s weapon list, 16 pure inference and marked as such.

**It is scored against human labels.** `swt.py`, `guns.py`,
`creature.py`, `enemyfields.py` and `charfile.py` carry field
labels rdr-fmt wrote. Held out as ground truth: **163 keys checked, 99 overlap, 64 do not (61%)**.

## Not every key is a description

**9184 keys are IDENTIFIERS** — `.rep`'s animation clip names,
`.flags`'s game-state flags, `.cam`'s camera names. They are
labels, not descriptions, and no gloss will make `_bod_rev_die`
mean something in prose. They are counted and set aside. The
**9211 descriptive keys** are what the table below covers; without
that split the headline reads 60% OPAQUE and is measuring the
wrong thing.

| confidence | keys | share | means |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **HIGH** | 4857 | 52.7% | every token is a word the game displays |
| **MEDIUM** | 505 | 5.5% | a token needed an abbreviation expansion |
| **OPAQUE** | 3849 | 41.8% | a token is neither a word nor a known abbreviation |

## The measured ceiling on this whole approach

**64 of the 163 keys with a hand-written label — 39% — have a
name that does not carry their meaning.** Not one of them is the
glosser misreading the name; the human simply knew something the
name does not say.

So: splitting a field name gets you something usable about 61%
of the time and misses real meaning the rest. **Do not ship these
as UI labels without a human reading them.** The 9048 descriptive
keys with no human label to check against should be assumed to
fail at the same rate.
